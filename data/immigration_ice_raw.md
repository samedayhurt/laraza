## 2025-11-25 — Federal Court Ruling Restricts Warrantless ICE Arrests Statewide
- **Date / trigger:** November 25, 2025 (66-page decision)
- **Case citation:** *Ramirez Ovando et al. v. Noem et al.*
- **Court:** U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado
- **Judge:** Senior U.S. District Judge R. Brooke Jackson
- **Summary:** Judge Jackson ruled ICE conduct "unlawful" and ordered agents to stop warrantless arrests without pre-arrest individualized assessment of flight risk. Key holding: "Mere presence within the United States in violation of United States immigration law is not, by itself, sufficient to conclude that a person is likely to escape before a warrant for arrest can be obtained." Ruling granted class-action status covering ALL individuals arrested since January 20, 2025. ICE must document specific facts supporting flight risk conclusion for each warrantless arrest. Named plaintiffs awarded bond refunds and ankle monitor removal.
- **Sources:** Colorado Sun (Nov 25, 2025); Denver Post (Nov 25, 2025); CPR (Nov 25, 2025); Westword (Nov 25, 2025).
- **Suggested response / referral:** Update all KYR cards with case citation *Ramirez Ovando v. Noem*; train rapid responders to cite ruling during ICE encounters; document any post-Nov 25 warrantless arrests where flight risk was not assessed—contact ACLU of Colorado immediately; inform detained individuals they may be eligible for bond refund and ankle monitor removal.

## 2025-09-08 — SCOTUS allows ethnicity/language/location as stop factors (*Noem v. Vasquez Perdomo*) — BACKFILL, KYR-critical
- **Date / trigger:** September 8, 2025 Supreme Court order (6-3, emergency docket; no full briefing, no oral argument, no majority opinion)
- **Agency or facility:** U.S. Supreme Court / DHS / ICE
- **Summary:** SCOTUS stayed a California district court injunction that had barred ICE "roving patrol" stops based on apparent ethnicity, Spanish or accented English, presence at a particular location, or type of work. Effect: those factors may be used as part of reasonable suspicion for an immigration stop. Still in force as of July 2026. This was missing from the repo baseline and it materially changes what KYR material should tell people: an ICE stop may be initiated on these grounds, so the practical protection is the right to remain silent and the right to decline consent — NOT an assurance that a stop based on appearance or language is itself unlawful.
- **Sources:** American Immigration Council, "How the Supreme Court's Latest Decision Clears the Way for Racial Profiling During Immigration Raids," https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/blog/supreme-courts-decision-racial-profiling-immigration-raids/ (accessed 2026-07-29); ACLU of Wisconsin, "Racial Profiling Rampant After Supreme Court Ruling," https://www.aclu-wi.org/news/racial-profiling-rampant-after-supreme-court-ruling/ (accessed 2026-07-29).
- **Suggested response / referral:** Correct any KYR handout that implies a stop based on appearance/language is per se unlawful. Reframe to: "You do not have to answer questions about where you were born or your immigration status. You can say 'I want to remain silent' and 'I do not consent to a search.'" Pair with *Ramirez Ovando* (below), which still constrains warrantless **arrest** in Colorado even where a stop is permitted.

## 2026-02-03 — Amended class complaint alleges ICE "field warrant" workaround and daily arrest quota
- **Date / trigger:** February 3, 2026 amended complaint in *Ramirez Ovando v. Noem*, D. Colo. 1:25-cv-03183-RBJ

## 2026-05-12 — Federal court finds ICE "materially violated" the Colorado warrantless-arrest injunction; orders retraining
- **Date / trigger:** May 12, 2026, 60-page order following a March 10-11, 2026 evidentiary hearing
- **Agency or facility:** ICE / U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado (Senior Judge R. Brooke Jackson)
- **Summary:** Judge Jackson found ICE **materially violated** both his November 25, 2025 preliminary injunction and federal law by continuing warrantless arrests without making and documenting the required flight-risk assessment. The court found affected people included parents of U.S.-citizen children, people with no criminal record, longtime residents, and asylum seekers. Remedies ordered: (1) ICE must develop compliance training within two weeks; (2) every immigration officer authorized to make warrantless arrests in Colorado must be trained within 45 days, and untrained officers — including new hires — are **barred from making warrantless arrests** until trained; (3) ICE must produce I-213 forms for warrantless arrests **and** for arrests on "field warrants issued after an encounter began"; (4) ICE must produce arrest lists (A-numbers, country of origin, arrest date, warrant status) for plaintiff-counsel review. As of the July 22, 2026 docket update a further motion to enforce remained under advisement.
- **Sources:** ACLU of Colorado, "Federal Judge Rules ICE Violated Court Order, Requires More Oversight and Training," https://www.aclu-co.org/press-releases/federal-judge-rules-ice-violated-court-order-requires-more-oversight-and-training/ (accessed 2026-07-29); CPR News, May 12 2026, https://www.cpr.org/2026/05/12/immigration-ice-judge-orders-more-training-warrentless-arrests/; Colorado Sun, May 12 2026, https://coloradosun.com/2026/05/12/ice-must-retrain-arresting-officers-in-colorado-federal-judge-rules/; Courthouse News Service, https://www.courthousenews.com/colorado-judge-forces-ice-to-train-agents-on-arrest-protocol/
- **Suggested response / referral:** This is the strongest current documentation tool. Rapid responders should record whether the arresting officer appeared to make any individualized flight-risk assessment, and whether a warrant existed **before** the encounter. Report suspected violations to ACLU of Colorado and CORRN (844-864-8341) — the court has an active compliance-monitoring channel, so contemporaneous reports have real leverage. Tell affected families the court is actively supervising ICE conduct in Colorado.

## 2026-05-26 — Courthouse arrest loophole: SB20-83 does not reach federal or combined courthouses
- **Date / trigger:** May 26, 2026 reporting on advocacy findings
- **Agency or facility:** ICE / Colorado state, county and municipal courthouses
- **Summary:** Colorado's SB20-83 bars civil immigration arrests inside and immediately outside county, municipal and state courthouses and protects people traveling to and from court; SB25-276 extended coverage to related treatment facilities. But the statute **does not apply to federal courthouses**, and advocates report ICE exploiting that gap at **combined buildings** that house both state and federal proceedings. CIRC also reports ICE reading court dockets for Spanish and Latin American surnames and then detaining people **days after** a court appearance — away from the courthouse, where SB20-83 does not protect them — including people who appeared as crime victims. Documented instances occurred in Glenwood Springs (inside a combined courthouse), Durango/La Plata County, Montezuma County (a car-accident victim whose name appeared on a docket), and Pagosa Springs. CORRN separately confirmed SB20-83 violations at Denver's Lindsey-Flannigan Courthouse. No Pueblo County Courthouse incident was confirmed in this reporting window, but the docket-harvesting pattern is not location-specific and Pueblo's courthouse should be treated as exposed.
- **Sources:** KSUT Public Radio, "Colorado advocates say ICE is abusing loopholes in a state law where they make arrests," May 26 2026, https://www.ksut.org/news/2026-05-26/colorado-advocates-say-ice-is-abusing-loopholes-in-a-state-law-where-they-make-arrests ; KDNK, Jun 10 2026, https://www.kdnk.org/rocky-mountain-community-radio/2026-06-10/colorado-advocates-say-ice-is-abusing-loopholes-in-a-state-law-where-they-make-arrests ; CIRC/CORRN statement, https://coloradoimmigrant.org/ ; Colorado Lawyers Committee SB20-83 page, https://coloradolawyerscommittee.org/immigration-ice-sb2083
- **Suggested response / referral:** Advise people with court dates to (1) ask counsel about **virtual appearance**, which CIRC now recommends; (2) request **court accompaniment** through CIRC; (3) understand that the risk window extends for **days after** the appearance, not just the courthouse visit itself; (4) confirm whether their courthouse is state-only or a combined state/federal building, because protection differs. Use the repo's Courthouse Monitoring Template and add a field for whether the building houses federal proceedings.

## 2026-05-13 — All three 2026 Colorado surveillance/location-data bills DIED; ICE access to local ALPR data still unrestricted
- **Date / trigger:** SB26-071 postponed indefinitely May 6 2026; HB26-1037 postponed indefinitely April 22 2026; SB26-070 laid over and lost when the General Assembly adjourned May 13 2026
- **Agency or facility:** Colorado General Assembly
- **Summary:** None of the three bills that would have limited government and ICE access to location and broker data became law.
  - **SB26-070** ("Ban Gov Access Historical Location Information Database," the "PEEPS Act") would have barred government entities from accessing databases revealing a person's or vehicle's historical location, barred sharing that data with third parties or out-of-jurisdiction agencies, excluded it from CORA, made improperly obtained data inadmissible, and given the AG enforcement power. It cleared Senate Judiciary and Appropriations in February 2026, was amended, then was laid over repeatedly in Senate second reading from April 23 and **died at adjournment on May 13 2026**.
  - **SB26-071** ("Use of Surveillance Technology by Law Enforcement," the SAFE Act) would have required warrants for facial recognition, restricted ALPR/traffic-camera use, capped retention, banned data sales, limited inter-agency sharing, and imposed biennial AG audits. **Postponed indefinitely 6-1 in Senate Judiciary on May 6 2026.**
  - **HB26-1037** ("Ban Gov Purchase of Personal Data from Third Party") would have barred government purchase of personal data from brokers and created a private right of action. **Postponed indefinitely in House Judiciary on April 22 2026.**
  **Consequence for immigration risk:** Colorado ALPR/Flock data held by local agencies remains subject only to each agency's own policy. ICE has no Flock contract, but the documented national pattern is ICE asking local agencies to run searches on its behalf — local and state police ran immigration-related ALPR searches over 4,000 times between June 2024 and May 2025 across networks covering 5,000+ communities. Flock added a one-toggle federal-sharing shutoff in January 2026 and a state-law disclaimer in February 2026, but both are **discretionary agency settings, not legal limits**. In Colorado, Boulder disabled national lookup in June 2025 and restricted sharing to in-state agencies — evidence that agency-level policy is the only operative control.
- **Sources:** Colorado General Assembly SB26-070, https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-070 ; SB26-071, https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-071 ; HB26-1037, https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1037 (all accessed 2026-07-29); Colorado Newsline, Feb 24 2026, https://coloradonewsline.com/2026/02/24/colorado-bill-limit-license-plate-reader-data/ ; EFF, "Are Your Local Police Using Flock Safety ALPRs to Scan for Immigrants?," Jun 2026, https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/06/are-your-local-police-using-flock-safety-alprs-scan-immigrants ; Boulder Reporting Lab, Aug 19 2025, https://boulderreportinglab.org/2025/08/19/until-recently-boulder-shared-license-plate-data-on-a-national-network-accessed-by-ice/
- **Suggested response / referral:** Because no state law now restrains it, the fight is local. File records requests with Pueblo PD and PCSO for (1) their ALPR audit logs, (2) whether federal sharing / national lookup is enabled, and (3) any immigration-related search requests received from federal agencies.
