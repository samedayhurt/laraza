## 2025-11-25 — Federal Court Ruling Restricts Warrantless ICE Arrests Statewide
- **Date / trigger:** November 25, 2025 (66-page decision)
- **Case citation:** *Ramirez Ovando et al. v. Noem et al.*
- **Court:** U.S. District Court for the District of Colorado
- **Judge:** Senior U.S. District Judge R. Brooke Jackson
- **Summary:** Judge Jackson ruled ICE conduct "unlawful" and ordered agents to stop warrantless arrests without pre-arrest individualized assessment of flight risk. Key holding: "Mere presence within the United States in violation of United States immigration law is not, by itself, sufficient to conclude that a person is likely to escape before a warrant for arrest can be obtained." Ruling granted class-action status covering ALL individuals arrested since January 20, 2025. ICE must document specific facts supporting flight risk conclusion for each warrantless arrest. Named plaintiffs awarded bond refunds and ankle monitor removal.
- **Sources:** Colorado Sun (Nov 25, 2025); Denver Post (Nov 25, 2025); CPR (Nov 25, 2025); Westword (Nov 25, 2025).
- **Suggested response / referral:** Update all KYR cards with case citation *Ramirez Ovando v. Noem*; train rapid responders to cite ruling during ICE encounters; document any post-Nov 25 warrantless arrests where flight risk was not assessed—contact ACLU of Colorado immediately; inform detained individuals they may be eligible for bond refund and ankle monitor removal.

## 2025-01 to 2025-11 — Statewide ICE Enforcement Surge: 300% Increase
- **Date / trigger:** January-November 2025 data analysis
- **Agency or facility:** ICE Denver Field Office
- **Summary:** ICE averaging 9 arrests per day in Colorado in 2025, representing 300% increase over 2024. ICE nearly doubled Colorado headcount during 2025 and actively recruiting more officers. ## 2025-08 — ICE Plans to Triple Colorado Detention Capacity, Including Walsenburg (50 Miles from Pueblo)
- **Date / trigger:** August 2025 (ICE planning documents published by Washington Post)
- **Agency or facility:** ICE / Aurora GEO Center / CoreCivic Walsenburg
- **Summary:** ICE plans to expand Aurora GEO facility from 1,360 to 1,530 beds and open three new detention centers: (1) Walsenburg Huerfano County Correctional Facility (1,400 beds, 50 miles south of Pueblo, owned by CoreCivic, dormant since 2010s); (2) Hudson facility near metro Denver; (3) Ignacio Southern Ute Detention Center (+28 beds). Total capacity increase from ~1,500 to over 4,000 detainees statewide. Aurora facility currently averaging 1,142 detainees daily (top 10 nationally). GEO Group investing $70 million for expansion. Walsenburg community has protested weekly since June 2025; 100+ protesters gathered August 15. Colorado's 2023 ban on state/local ICE contracts does NOT apply to privately owned facilities.
- **Sources:** Colorado Newsline (2025); CPR "Dozens protest potential ICE detention facility near Walsenburg" (Aug 15, 2025); Axios Denver (Mar 3, 2025).
- **Suggested response / referral:** Monitor Walsenburg facility opening timeline (50 miles from Pueblo); coordinate with Walsenburg protesters for regional solidarity actions; if Walsenburg opens, establish RMIAN intake protocol for Pueblo-area families detained there; map transportation routes between Pueblo and Walsenburg for family visitation support.
