## 2026-02-09 — City Council passed resolutions opposing three state surveillance/privacy bills
- **Source / meeting link:** Pueblo Chieftain, "Why Pueblo city council is against these 3 state bills" (Feb 11 2026, chieftain.com/story/news/2026/02/11/why-pueblo-city-council-is-against-these-3-state-bills/88604786007/); preview coverage at Yahoo/USA Today Network (yahoo.com/news/articles/know-pueblo-city-council-meeting-143018079.html); meeting video on City of Pueblo Facebook (facebook.com/CityofPueblo/videos/pueblo-city-council/2145740586181846/).
- **Key takeaways:**
  - **SB26-070 ("PEEPS Act") — Ban Gov Access to Historical Location Information Database.** City passed a resolution *opposing*. Bill would prohibit government entities from accessing databases revealing historical location information for individuals or vehicles (with exceptions). Directly targets ALPR/Flock historical-query workflows and related third-party location data. See leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-070.
  - **SB26-071 — Use of Surveillance Technology by Law Enforcement.** City passed a resolution *opposing*. Bill would require law enforcement to use surveillance tech only for lawful purposes tied to public safety or active investigations, and imposes collection/storage/sharing/destruction rules on collected data. See leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-071.
  - **HB26-1037 — Third-party data collection for government use.** City passed a resolution *opposing*, characterizing the bill in its own resolution language as **"harmful to public safety"** (the one direct phrase carried through in press coverage).
  - **Context:** Pueblo has an operational RTCC stack (Daktronics/High Point + ShotSpotter + Flock ALPR corridors + Genetec Community Connect). All three bills would constrain tools the city's current surveillance posture depends on — so the opposition tracks with the 5-2 conservative council majority's procurement history.
  - **CML alignment (SB26-070):** Colorado Municipal League's position paper argues the bill's 72-hour warrant requirement is impractical, its 30-day deletion rule for traffic-cam data conflicts with court-proceeding needs, it removes public-record exclusions, and its supervisor-approval/logging/auditing/training/reporting stack is "burdensome and costly." Pueblo's opposition appears to echo CML framing rather than stake out an independent position. (cml.org position paper.)

## 2026-05-06 — SB26-071 (SAFE Act) postponed indefinitely 6-1 in Senate Judiciary
- **Source / meeting link:** https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-071 — status **Lost**. History: introduced 01/28/2026, assigned to Senate Judiciary; **02/23/2026** "Lay Over Unamended — Amendment(s) Failed"; **05/06/2026 Postpone Indefinitely, 6-1**. - **Key takeaways:**
  - **Final disposition: dead, formally killed in committee 6-1 on May 6 2026** — a week before sine die and a week after SB26-070 was pulled. Prime sponsor **Sen. Lynda Zamora Wilson (R-Colorado Springs)**, with no listed co-sponsors on the leg.colorado.gov page (thinner support than SB26-070).
  - Full name was the **"Surveillance Accountability and Freedom Ensured (SAFE) Act."** Colorado Politics also attributes its death to governor veto threats.
  - **Had it passed, its effective date would have been July 1 2027** — worth noting, because a 2027 reintroduction on the same timeline would land squarely in Pueblo's next RTCC contract-renewal cycle.

Its death means none of those are subject to a state published-use-policy or audit mandate.

## 2026-04-22 — HB26-1037 postponed indefinitely 6-5 in House Judiciary
- **Source / meeting link:** https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/HB26-1037 — full title **"Ban Government Purchase of Personal Data from Third Party"**; status **Lost (Postponed Indefinitely)**. History: introduced 01/14/2026 to House Judiciary; **02/25/2026** witness testimony and discussion; **04/22/2026** committee adopted amendment L.009 **6-5**, then a motion to refer to Appropriations **failed 5-6**, then a motion to **postpone indefinitely passed 6-5**.
- **Key takeaways:**

## 2026-05-13 — Net effect for Pueblo: all three bills dead, no state guardrails, but two new laws DO bind local agencies
- **Source / meeting link:** Colorado General Assembly 2026 regular session adjourned sine die **May 13 2026** (per HB26-1276 effective-date arithmetic, https://detention-pipeline.transparencycascade.org/entry/colorado-state-pushback-ice-detention-2026/). - **Key takeaways:**
  - **The headline for this repo: Pueblo City Council opposed three bills and all three died. Colorado ended the 2026 session with no statute regulating ALPRs, no statute regulating law-enforcement surveillance technology generally, and no ban on government purchase of third-party personal data.** Every constraint on the Pueblo RTCC/Flock/ShotSpotter/drone stack is therefore local and discretionary. Do not describe Pueblo's opposition as symbolic — it aligned with the winning coalition.
  - **Two 2026 laws that DID pass and DO create concrete local obligations:**
    - **HB26-1123 (Preventing Sexual Abuse in Jails)** — **signed May 27 2026, effective May 27 2026** (safety clause). https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb26-1123 . Sponsors Reps. Katie Stewart & Javier Mabrey, Sens. Judy Amabile & Mike Weissman. Prompted by a former La Plata County jail commander allegedly using jail surveillance footage to watch strip searches of 100+ women. **Requires detention personnel to record strip searches on body-worn camera; requires the sheriff to coordinate with the BWC vendor to create a strip-search tag and apply "the most restrictive level of access available"; requires each facility to document the number, reason and result of every strip search in an annual report to the jail standards advisory committee and the Colorado Attorney General.** This lands directly on **PCSO's brand-new 672-bed detention center** and creates a real, dated paper trail that can be requested.
    - **HB26-1276 (Protect Safety of Individuals Who Are Immigrants)** — cleared the legislature **May 11 2026**, **signed by Gov. Polis June 4 2026**, **effective Aug 12 2026** (no safety clause; 90 days post-adjournment). https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb26-1276 . Provisions relevant to Pueblo: **unannounced state inspections of immigration detention facilities at least four times per year** (food/water standards, confinement conditions, detainee care); **civil liability extended to employers whose staff disclose a person's immigration status to federal authorities**; **POST Board must train officers on federal immigration law**; and the **Attorney General must set policy on sharing personally identifiable information**. That last item is the one hook that partially substitutes for what SB26-070 would have done on data sharing — worth watching what the AG issues.
  - **Also notable:** **HB26-1210** (ban on using surveillance data to set individualized prices/wages) **passed and was VETOED by Polis** — a second 2026 data point that this Governor vetoes or threatens to veto surveillance-limiting bills. **SB26-189** (notice when AI is used in college/job/loan decisions) was signed. **HB26-1009** requires officers on domestic-violence calls to assess lethality risk and refer high-risk victims to a community advocate, with annual data reporting.
  - **The Polis pattern is the strategic finding.** Three surveillance-limiting measures died at least partly on veto threat, and a fourth was actually vetoed. Nguyen's "probably with a new governor" line is the tell: **Polis is term-limited and the Nov 2026 governor's race is the upstream variable** for any 2027 ALPR statute.

## 2026-07-11 — Pueblo County's new 672-bed detention center fully occupied; capacity and expansion room now exist
- **Source / meeting link:** KKTV, "Pueblo opens new detention center, 150 more beds, first net-zero energy jail in the US," May 8 2026, https://www.kktv.com/2026/05/08/pueblo-opens-new-detention-center-150-more-beds-first-net-zero-energy-jail-us/ ; KKTV, "All Pueblo County inmates transferred to new detention facility," Jul 11 2026, https://www.kktv.com/2026/07/11/all-pueblo-county-inmates-transferred-new-detention-facility/ ; KOAA, "Pueblo County opens new detention center with 672 beds, solar energy design," https://www.koaa.com/news/local-news/pueblo-county-opens-new-detention-center-with-672-beds-solar-energy-design ; Daily Colorado News, Jun 15 2026, https://dailycoloradonews.com/pueblo-county-commissioners-advance-180m-jail-plan/ ; Pueblo County Correctional Services, https://county.pueblo.org/correctional-services/correctional-services
- **Key takeaways:**
  - The new facility at **3830 Medal of Honor Blvd** on Pueblo's west side opened in early **May 2026**; **all inmates were transferred by Jul 11 2026**. **672 beds, ~150 more than the 1980-built downtown jail**, on a **33-acre** site, marketed as the first **net-zero-energy** jail in the US with an on-site solar array.
  - **~$180 million project** — described as the largest capital construction effort in Pueblo County history — funded with roughly **$125 million in certificates of participation (COPs)**, serviced by a **2016 sales tax increase** and the **2019 retail marijuana sales tax increase (3.5% → 6%)** that commissioners designated for the jail. **No new property tax.** COPs are a lease-purchase structure that does **not** require voter approval, which is worth stating plainly in any public explainer.
  - Driver was **chronic overcrowding** — populations exceeding design capacity by upwards of 200 at times — plus **state authorities threatening sanctions** if the county did not come into compliance.
  - **Sheriff Lucero at the opening: "We do have the ability out here if we come close to capacity at 680, that we can then expand on this 33 acres to bring another cell block in,"** and "We can do more things that we were not able to do or accomplish in our old structure."
  - **Why this belongs in the power map: surplus detention capacity plus explicit, sheriff-stated expansion room is the precondition for federal bed-rental contracts.** ~150 net new beds arrive in the same year that ICE is reported to be seeking additional Colorado facilities, and the decision-maker changes hands in January 2027. No source states that Pueblo County holds or seeks any ICE/USMS contract — **this is a risk to monitor, not a finding.** Note also that Colorado law bars local governments from contracting with **private** civil immigration detention facilities (cited in Councilor Flores' Nov 2025 resolution); a **county-run** facility is a different legal question.
  - **HB26-1123 (effective May 27 2026) now applies to this facility** — BWC recording of strip searches, restricted-access tagging, and an annual report to the jail standards advisory committee and the Attorney General. New facility, new statutory reporting duty, brand-new document trail.
  - **HB26-1276 (effective Aug 12 2026)** mandates unannounced state inspections at least four times a year of *immigration* detention facilities — a tripwire that would attach if Pueblo County ever housed immigration detainees.
- **Gaps / what I couldn't confirm:** Whether the facility holds or plans to hold any federal/ICE/USMS detainees or has an intergovernmental agreement to do so — **no source confirms or denies; explicitly unresolved.** Bed count is reported as both 627 and 672 across outlets (KOAA/KKTV say 672; Daily Colorado News says ~627) — **use 672 with the discrepancy noted.** The commissioners' roll call on the jail contracts. Surveillance technology installed inside the facility. Daily Colorado News is an outlet I have not vetted; its cost/COP figures are uncorroborated by the broadcast outlets.
  - Also unresolved: **Colorado Times Recorder (Mar 2026) reports ICE "hold rooms" exist in locations "from Craig to Pueblo"** (https://coloradotimesrecorder.com/2026/03/exclusive-secret-ice-detention-facilities-exist-around-colorado-data-shows/76983/). I did not verify the Pueblo specifics — location, operator, whether it is in a county facility.
