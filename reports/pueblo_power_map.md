# Pueblo Power Map

Pueblo’s governing landscape shifted sharply in 2024–25, giving conservative blocs control over both city council and the county commission just as surveillance investments ramped up. This map highlights who holds the pen on budgets and permits, plus concrete ways to confront them with conflicts-of-interest and public pressure.

## Executive Summary
- **City Council:** Five of seven seats now align with conservative priorities, approving surveillance tech, curfews, and restrictive protest permits despite the “nonpartisan” ballot label.[^maes]
- **County Commission:** Two of three commissioners (Paula McPheeters, Zach Swearingen) are Republicans with business ties to higher-ed grants and firearms/fitness ventures, granting them sway over sheriff, RTCC, and ALPR contracts.[^bartolo]
- **Countywide offices:** Clerk Candace Rivera, Sheriff David Lucero, and DA Kala Beauvais influence transparency (election canvassing), detention policy, and prosecution choices. Their public statements should anchor advocacy scripts pulled into each report section.
- **Governance form:** Pueblo runs a strong-mayor / council system with **seven council seats — four elected by district (Districts 1–4) and three elected at large**, one at-large seat serving as Council President. (A previous version of this report said "nine districts"; that was wrong, and it never matched the 5-2 majority arithmetic used elsewhere in this file.) Ballots are labeled nonpartisan, but alignments track partisan priorities, so treat every seat as a potential swing vote on surveillance budgets.
- **Economic inequity context:** ACS 2024 5-year: city median household income **$56,664** with **17.5%** in poverty (county **$64,010 / 14.6%**), underscoring why surveillance funding vs. community investment debates should reference census stats.[^census] Note these poverty rates are **not** comparable to the figures in earlier versions of this report — the older ZIP-level percentages were mis-derived from a Census *count* column and were internally impossible. See `data/pueblo_context_raw_notes.md` (2026-07-29) before citing any trend.

## Conflict & Accountability Tracker
- **Republic Shooting Range / The Beach Fitness:** Co-founded by Commissioner Swearingen; flag any firearms training or wellness contracts.
- **Pueblo Community College ties:** Commissioner McPheeters oversees grant compliance; scrutinize education-related procurement for favoritism.
- **Railroad technology interests:** Commissioner Miles Lucero’s professional background suggests potential overlaps in infrastructure projects.
- **ICE cooperation pledge:** Sheriff David Lucero’s Jan 23, 2025 statement limits PCSO participation in ICE “roundups.” Track agendas that contradict that pledge (detention zones, ICE MOUs) and cite the statement in complaints.
- **Union litigation:** Sheriff’s lawsuit against Colorado DOL/union rules could influence surveillance staffing priorities—flag conflicts if anti-union moves coincide with tech purchases.
- **Voter rights:** Clerk Candace Rivera’s accelerated canvass demonstrates effective election oversight; resist any budget cuts or restrictions that threaten this progress.
Capture these leads in `data/pueblo_politics_raw_notes.md` and prep pointed testimony/questions ahead of agenda items.

## Action Queue
1. **Sunday agenda audit:** Pull City Council + County Commission agendas weekly; highlight items referencing surveillance, ALPR, procurement over $250k, or charter changes.[^watchlist]
2. **Conflict question scripts:** Draft two targeted questions per official before meetings (e.g., contract beneficiaries, data-sharing rules) and share them with public commenters.
3. **CORA prep:** Maintain ready-to-send CORA templates for RTCC vendor contracts, IA statistics, and ALPR placement maps; file immediately after votes.
