# Pueblo Resources Guide

Community safety in Pueblo depends on quick access to trusted legal, housing, health, and survivor services. Use this guide to match urgent needs with organizations already operating in the county, and pair every outreach with documentation (names, dates, case IDs) so follow-up teams can advocate effectively.

## Executive Summary
- Civil legal aid: **Colorado Legal Services (CLS)** runs the Southeastern Colorado office in downtown Pueblo, handling eviction defense, protection orders, and limited immigration screenings for income-qualified residents.[^cls]
- Housing & homelessness: **Posada** coordinates motel vouchers, mail services, and supportive housing; partner site **SafeSide Recovery** doubled shelter capacity with eight new dorm-style units in Jan 2026.[^posada][^safeside]
- Survivor safety: **YWCA Pueblo** (domestic violence) and **Rape Crisis Services** offer 24/7 hotlines, shelter/accompaniment, and bilingual advocates.[^ywca][^rcs]
- Mental health: **Health Solutions’ Crisis Walk-In Center** provides no-cost stabilization, mobile crisis, and detox linkages regardless of insurance.[^hs]

## Self-Advocacy Moves
1. **Document eligibility on intake.** Capture names, date/time, staff contact, and any case/reference numbers the organization provides so future volunteers can check status without repeating the story.
2. **Pair legal + social services.** When CLS opens an eviction or DV case, loop in Posada or YWCA for housing safety planning so legal timelines don’t leave families without shelter.
3. **Hotline call scripts.** Before dialing YWCA or Rape Crisis Services, write down a 3-line script (who needs help, immediate safety risks, callback number) to move through triage faster.
4. **Mental health + protest support.** For demonstrations, stage a Health Solutions crisis-line card at the medic table so participants know where to seek 24/7 stabilization if trauma surfaces later.
5. **Track referrals centrally.** Update the Obsidian vault or encrypted spreadsheet with every referral (date, org, outcome) to measure gaps and escalate to funders or officials when capacity runs out.
