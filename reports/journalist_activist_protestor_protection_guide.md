# Journalist, Activist, and Protestor Protection Guide

This guide distills the power, surveillance, and resource research into actionable steps for anyone documenting or participating in Pueblo actions—journalists, legal observers, mutual-aid leads, and frontline activists.

## Who This Serves
- **Journalists & documentarians** covering city council, protests, or community safety.
- **Activists & organizers** coordinating marches, canvasses, or rapid-response calls.
- **Legal observers & community medics** who need to document abuse and route survivors to help.

## Top Risks Right Now
1. **RTCC omnipresence:** Multi-sensor monitoring (ShotSpotter, bodycams, drones, ALPR) lets PPD cross-reference footage quickly; assume march routes are tracked in real time.[^surv]
2. **Council/commission power shift:** Conservative majorities fast-track surveillance budgets and curfews, limiting protest permits and appeal options unless challenged publicly.[^power]
3. **Rumor-driven ICE panic:** Viral posts about “roundups” trigger fear and distraction; only verified calls via CORRN should guide mobilizations.[^imm]
4. **Resource exhaustion:** Shelter beds, legal aid, and crisis services have limited capacity—without documentation of referrals, people fall through gaps.[^res]

## Self-Advocacy & Safety Moves
### Before the Action
- **Map exposure:** Review `docs/maps/pueblo-surveillance-map.html` to identify camera clusters and ALPR corridors; adjust ingress/egress routes accordingly.
- **Prep case IDs:** Use `YYYYMMDD-Event-Initials` across notebooks, audio, and media hashes so evidence remains synchronized (per `opsec/Legal Observer Toolkit.md`).
- **Know your officials:** Print the power map table with conflict questions (e.g., Swearingen’s business ties) and assign speakers to raise them during public comment.

### During the Action
- **Document + hash:** Record short clips focused on badges; run `sha256sum` on the curb and text hashes to legal channels so IA/POST packets are ready if violence occurs.
- **Verify ICE/POLice rumors:** When alerts surface, collect license plates, addresses, and agent descriptions, then call CORRN 844-864-8341 opt.1 before blasting Signal chats.
- **Deploy resources on site:** Keep copies of the Pueblo Resources Guide table so medics/legal can hand out cards for CLS, YWCA, Rape Crisis Services, Health Solutions, and Posada.

### After the Action
- **File complaints fast:** Submit IA forms the same day, then escalate via DA/POST/AG if responses stall; log case numbers in the Obsidian vault alongside media hashes.
- **Track referrals:** Update the shared resource log with who contacted which org and the outcome (accepted, waitlisted, no response).
- **Debrief + update data:** Append new insights to `data/*_raw` files (politics votes, sensor sightings, ICE incidents) so the next sprint starts from a higher baseline.
