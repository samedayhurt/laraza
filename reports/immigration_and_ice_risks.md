# Immigration and ICE Risks

*Updated: **July 29, 2026***

Pueblo residents face escalating federal enforcement, evolving county policies, and new surveillance threats. This brief captures the latest intelligence and tools for asserting rights and documenting abuses.

## Executive Summary
- **The Colorado injunction is in force — and has been enforced.** *Ramirez Ovando v. Noem* (Nov 25, 2025) restricted ICE warrantless arrests. On **May 12, 2026** the court found ICE **"materially violated"** it, ordered retraining within 45 days, and **barred untrained officers** from warrantless arrests.[^court] ⚠️ **It is on appeal** (10th Cir. No. 26-1027), **undecided, with no stay.** Cite it as *in effect, on appeal* — never as settled law.
- **A second ruling cuts the other way, and the community needs to know it.** ***Noem v. Vasquez Perdomo*** (SCOTUS, Sept 8, 2025) allows ethnicity, Spanish/accented English, location, and type of work to factor into reasonable suspicion for a **stop**. A stop is not an arrest — but do not teach people that a stop based on appearance is automatically unlawful.
- **Enforcement did not recede in 2026:** roughly **12 arrests per day** statewide held through March 10, 2026, and **65% had no prior conviction**. Pueblo County remains among Colorado's highest-arrest counties.
- **Sheriff Lucero reaffirmed his pledge (Jan 29, 2026) — then lost the June 30, 2026 primary.** **Allen Medina** takes office **January 2027** unopposed. The pledge this brief relies on belongs to an outgoing officeholder, is not law, and Medina has not addressed surveillance technology on the record.
- **Walsenburg did NOT open as ICE detention.** The state's purchase was rejected by the Joint Budget Committee on **March 31, 2026**; no CoreCivic–ICE contract exists; a zoning measure goes to Walsenburg voters **Nov 3, 2026**. Base capacity is **752**, not 1,400 (that figure is a double-bunked planning maximum).
- **Capacity grew elsewhere instead:** **GEO won a five-year, 1,200-bed ICE contract at Hudson on July 14, 2026** (~140 mi from Pueblo), taking statewide capacity to roughly **2,700 beds**.
- **An unlisted ICE hold room serves the Pueblo area (facility code PUEHOLD)** — 315 people held Jan–Oct 2025, including stays of ~12, 14, and 19 days. **It is not in the city of Pueblo**; it is near the Fremont County airport. **Absence from ICE's online detainee locator is not evidence someone is not detained.**
- **No state-law backstop was created.** All three 2026 surveillance bills died and **SB26-005 was vetoed June 3, 2026** — so do **not** claim a state-court remedy against ICE agents exists, and do **not** claim Colorado limits ICE access to local ALPR data. **HB26-1276** (signed June 4, effective **Aug 12, 2026**) is the one surviving lever: quarterly unannounced detention inspections, POST immigration training, and an AG-set policy on PII sharing.
- **Sensitive areas remain rescinded:** ICE can enforce at schools, churches, and hospitals (Jan 21, 2025).[^sensitive]
- **Social media surveillance:** ICE seeking 24/7 monitoring of Facebook, TikTok, WhatsApp, Instagram — friends/relatives may be tracked.[^social]

## Critical Legal Update: the injunction, and its limits

**What the court did.** On November 25, 2025, Senior U.S. District Judge R. Brooke Jackson issued a 66-page ruling finding ICE had "routinely carried out illegal arrests" in Colorado, and enjoined warrantless arrests based solely on suspected immigration violations.

**What happened next.** The government appealed on **January 23, 2026** (10th Cir. No. 26-1027; opening brief June 1, 2026). **No stay was entered, so the injunction remained in force throughout.** On **May 12, 2026**, after noncompliance reports, the court found ICE had **"materially violated"** the order and required: training within two weeks, all Colorado warrantless-arrest officers trained within 45 days, **untrained officers barred from warrantless arrests**, production of I-213 forms for warrantless arrests *and* for "field warrants issued after an encounter began," and arrest lists for plaintiffs' counsel. A further enforcement motion was under advisement as of the July 22, 2026 docket.

**How to use this — and how not to:**
- ✅ Say: *"A federal court order restricts warrantless ICE arrests in Colorado. I want to remain silent. I want a lawyer."*
- ❌ Do **not** say "ICE cannot arrest me" or present the ruling as final. It is on appeal and could be narrowed or overturned.
- ⚠️ **Re-verify the 10th Circuit docket before any reprint of this brief or the KYR handouts.** A confident claim that stops being true is worse than a hedged one.
- Document every encounter and contact RMIAN or ACLU of Colorado immediately.

## Pueblo County Jail Policy

- PCSO does NOT proactively notify ICE of immigration status
- Only responded to ONE ICE inquiry in 2024
- ICE must provide warrant/writ signed by federal judge to request hold
- Cannot detain people after release solely for ICE pickup
- Fingerprints go to FBI, which automatically shares with ICE

## New Threats in 2025

### ICE 24/7 Social Media Monitoring
ICE published RFI for contractors to monitor: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, Twitter/X, WhatsApp, YouTube, Reddit, LinkedIn, and more.

**Risks:**
- Posts can be used to identify, locate, and build cases
- Friends and relatives of targets may be monitored
- Location data from posts reveals movement patterns

**Protective actions:**
- Review privacy settings immediately on all platforms
- Remove location data from posts
- Use Signal instead of WhatsApp for sensitive communications
- Warn community members about social media risks

### ICE Sensitive Areas Policy Rescinded
As of January 21, 2025, ICE can conduct enforcement at:
- Schools
- Places of worship
- Hospitals and medical facilities

**Response:** Train staff at schools and churches on Know Your Rights protocols. Develop rapid response plans for these locations.

### Detention landscape (corrected July 2026)

**Walsenburg / Huerfano County — did NOT open as ICE detention.**
- Located in Walsenburg, ~50 miles south of Pueblo; base capacity **752** (the 1,400 figure circulating earlier is a double-bunked planning maximum, not beds).
- The State's **$150–200M purchase was rejected by the Joint Budget Committee on March 31, 2026.** No CoreCivic–ICE contract exists as of July 2026.
- **A zoning ballot measure goes to Walsenburg voters on November 3, 2026** — that is the decision point to organize around, not a done deal.

**Hudson — this is where capacity actually grew.**
- **GEO Group announced a five-year, 1,200-bed ICE contract on July 14, 2026** (~$85M/yr), roughly **140 miles** from Pueblo. Statewide ICE capacity is now about **2,700 beds**.

**Aurora (~120 mi)** — an active tuberculosis case was confirmed July 2026, and GEO obstructed the state investigation that Colorado law requires access for. Relevant to any detained-person welfare check.

**PUEHOLD — an unlisted hold room serving the Pueblo area.**
- **315 people held Jan–Oct 2025**, including stays of roughly **12, 14, and 19 days** — well beyond a short processing hold.
- **It is not located in the city of Pueblo.** Reporting places it near the Fremont County airport.
- ⚠️ **It does not appear in ICE's public detainee locator.** Teach people that a locator search returning nothing is **not** proof someone isn't detained. Keep calling CORRN and RMIAN.

**Pueblo Memorial Airport** — community protests over potential ICE transport use; still not confirmed as an active ICE site. Worth watching, not asserting.

## Know Your Rights

**If ICE comes to your door:**
1. You do NOT have to open the door
2. Ask to see a warrant signed by a judge (not just ICE paperwork)
3. You have the right to remain silent
4. Do not sign any documents without an attorney
5. Memorize: **CORRN 844-864-8341**

**Constitutional rights:**
- Fifth Amendment: Right to remain silent
- Right to attorney before answering questions
- Do not grant access to property without valid warrant
