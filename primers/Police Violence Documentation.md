# Police Violence Documentation Primer

How to safely document, preserve, and report police violence in Colorado. This guide covers evidence collection, medical protocols, and pathways to accountability.

---

## Why Documentation Matters

Good documentation can:
- Protect you in court
- Support civil rights lawsuits
- Create pattern evidence for AG investigations
- Help others who face similar treatment
- Hold officers accountable through POST decertification

**Without documentation, it's your word against theirs.**

---

## During the Incident

### Priority: SURVIVE FIRST

```
IMMEDIATE SAFETY
================

1. DO NOT physically resist - even if force is unjustified
2. Protect your head and vital organs if being struck
3. Try to remain conscious and aware
4. If possible, go limp rather than tense up
5. Call out loudly: "I AM NOT RESISTING"
6. Witnesses: RECORD but do NOT intervene physically
```

### Mental Documentation (When Recording Isn't Possible)

If you can't record, memorize:

| What to Note | Why It Matters |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Badge numbers** | Identifies specific officers |
| **Patrol car numbers** | Backup identification |
| **Time** | Establishes timeline |
| **Location** | Scene reconstruction |
| **Officer descriptions** | Height, weight, hair, distinguishing features |
| **What was said** | Exact words, tone, threats |
| **Weapons used** | Taser, baton, firearm, chemical agents |
| **Your injuries** | What hurts, where, how severe |
| **Witness faces** | May need to find them later |

### If You CAN Record

- **Start recording immediately** - Colorado protects this right
- **State the date, time, and location** on recording
- **Narrate what you see** if witnessing
- **Capture badge numbers and faces**
- **Don't stop recording** until incident ends
- **Back up to cloud immediately** after

## Immediately After (First 24 Hours)

### Medical Documentation

**GO TO THE HOSPITAL** - Even for "minor" injuries:

1. Tell staff you were injured by police
2. Request **complete examination and documentation**
3. Ask for copies of all medical records
4. Photograph your injuries at the hospital (with date visible)
5. Get contact info for medical staff who treated you

```
INJURIES THAT MAY NOT APPEAR IMMEDIATELY
========================================

• Bruising - May take 24-72 hours to fully show
• Internal bleeding - May not be obvious
• Concussion symptoms - Can worsen over days
• Soft tissue damage - Swelling develops over time
• Psychological trauma - PTSD symptoms may be delayed

RETURN TO HOSPITAL if symptoms worsen or new symptoms appear
```

### Photograph Everything

**Take photos of:**
- All visible injuries (day of AND following days)
- Torn/damaged clothing
- Personal property damage
- Scene of incident (if safe to return)
- Any evidence left behind (blood, spent cartridges, etc.)

**Photo best practices:**
- Include something for scale (ruler, coin, hand)
- Take multiple angles
- Use good lighting
- Include timestamp (phone settings)
- Store in multiple locations

### Write It Down

**Within 24 hours, write a detailed account:**

```
INCIDENT REPORT TEMPLATE
========================

DATE/TIME of incident:
LOCATION (specific address/intersection):
MY ACTIVITIES prior to police contact:
HOW POLICE APPROACHED (on foot, vehicle, lights/sirens):
WHAT POLICE SAID (exact words if possible):
WHAT I SAID (exact words):
PHYSICAL FORCE USED (describe each action):
WEAPONS USED (taser, baton, chemical spray, firearm):
MY INJURIES (describe each, location on body):
OFFICER IDENTIFIERS:
  - Badge numbers:
  - Names (if stated):
  - Physical descriptions:
  - Patrol car numbers:
WITNESSES (names, contact info, what they saw):
OUTCOME (arrest? citation? released?):
MEDICAL TREATMENT RECEIVED:
FOLLOW-UP SYMPTOMS:

Written by: [Your name]
Date written: [Date]
```

## Evidence Preservation

### Chain of Custody

For legal proceedings, you must show evidence wasn't tampered with:

1. **Save originals** - Never edit source files
2. **Create copies** with hashes (sha256sum)
3. **Document where/when** you saved each copy
4. **Store in multiple locations:**
   - Cloud backup (encrypted)
   - External hard drive
   - USB drive in secure location
   - With trusted attorney

### Digital Evidence

```
PRESERVING DIGITAL EVIDENCE
===========================

□ Don't delete ANYTHING from your phone
□ Back up entire phone to computer
□ Save cloud copies of all relevant files
□ Screenshot relevant social media posts
□ Download body cam footage requests
□ Print key documents (paper backup)
□ Generate hashes of video/audio files:
  $ sha256sum filename.mp4 > filename.sha256
```

### Physical Evidence

- **Don't wash clothes** - blood, chemical residue is evidence
- **Bag clothing separately** in paper bags (not plastic)
- **Label each bag** with date, your name, item description
- **Photograph items before bagging**
- **Store in cool, dry location**

## Reporting Pathways

### Step 1: Pueblo PD Internal Affairs

**File first, even if you don't expect results - it creates a record.**

- **Deadline:** 60 days from incident
- **Online:** pueblo.us/449/Internal-Affairs-Section
- **Phone:** 719-553-2412
- **In person:** 200 S. Main St, Pueblo, CO 81003

**What to include:**
- Your written account (see template above)
- Photos of injuries
- Names/badge numbers of officers
- Witness contact information
- Video/audio evidence (reference, don't send originals)

**Request:**
- Written confirmation of complaint receipt
- Complaint number for tracking
- Timeline for investigation
- Written outcome when concluded

### Step 2: Colorado POST

For serious misconduct (excessive force, civil rights violations):

- **Website:** post.colorado.gov/certification-inquiries-officer-complaints
- **Can result in:** Officer decertification (can't work in CO law enforcement)

**POST investigates:**
- Use of excessive force
- Unlawful arrests
- Dishonesty/falsifying reports
- Failure to intervene when witnessing misconduct
- Pattern of complaints

### Step 3: Colorado Attorney General Pattern & Practice

For systemic civil rights violations:

- **Website:** coag.gov/office-sections/civil-rights/pattern-and-practice/
- **Select:** "Governmental Authority"
- **Provide:** Your incident + any knowledge of similar incidents to others

**AG investigates patterns of:**
- Excessive force
- Discriminatory policing
- Unconstitutional policies
- Failure to discipline officers

### Step 4: Federal Department of Justice

For serious federal civil rights violations:

- **Website:** civilrights.justice.gov/report/
- **FBI Civil Rights:** Contact local FBI field office

**Federal jurisdiction when:**
- Constitutional rights violated "under color of law"
- Pattern of civil rights violations
- Hate crimes involved

### Step 5: Civil Lawsuit

**Colorado removed qualified immunity for state claims (SB20-217)**

Contact:
- **ACLU Colorado:** 303-777-5482
- **Colorado Legal Services:** 719-545-6708
- **Civil rights attorneys** (many work on contingency)

**Potential damages:**
- Medical expenses
- Lost wages
- Pain and suffering
- Punitive damages
- Attorney's fees

## Trauma and Self-Care

### Recognize Trauma Responses

After police violence, you may experience:

- **Flashbacks** or intrusive memories
- **Hypervigilance** (constantly alert to danger)
- **Avoidance** of places/situations
- **Sleep problems** and nightmares
- **Anger** or emotional numbness
- **Physical symptoms** (headaches, nausea)

**These are NORMAL responses to abnormal events.**

### Getting Support

| Resource | Contact | Services |
|----------|---------|----------|
| **Colorado Crisis Services** | 844-493-8255 | 24/7 mental health |
| **Health Solutions Crisis** | 844-493-8255 | Walk-in crisis care |
| **Pueblo Community Health** | (719) 543-8711 | Behavioral health |

### Community Support

- **Talk to trusted people** about what happened
- **Connect with advocacy groups** (ACLU, NLG, community organizations)
- **Attend support groups** for survivors of police violence
- **Take breaks** from re-living the experience
- **Exercise your agency** by pursuing accountability
