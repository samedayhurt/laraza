# Youth and Family Digital Safety

Protecting children and teenagers from digital surveillance, school monitoring, predatory apps, and law enforcement encounters. Includes guidance for immigrant families.

---

## School Surveillance: What Parents Need to Know

### Your Child Is Being Monitored

If your child uses a school-issued device (laptop, Chromebook, iPad), their activity is almost certainly being monitored. Millions of students are subject to AI-powered surveillance.

### Common Monitoring Software

| Software | What It Does | Used By |
|----------|--------------|---------|
| **Gaggle** | Scans documents, emails, chat for keywords | 1,500+ districts |
| **Securly** | Filters web, monitors social media | 10,000+ schools |
| **GoGuardian** | Screen monitoring, location tracking | 12,000+ schools |
| **Bark** | Social media + device monitoring | Schools & parents |
| **Lightspeed** | Web filtering, screen capture | Common in CO |

### What Gets Flagged

```
SCHOOL MONITORING TRIGGERS
==========================

These systems use AI to flag:

• "Self-harm" keywords (including song lyrics, creative writing)
• "Violence" references (including video game discussions)
• Profanity and "inappropriate" language
• LGBTQ+ content (disproportionately flagged)
• Political content
• Searches about drugs, alcohol
• Mental health research
• Communications with unknown contacts
```

### The Problems with School Monitoring

**No evidence it works**: A 2023 RAND study found only "scant evidence" of benefits from AI school surveillance. No independent research shows it reduces violence or suicide.

**Disproportionate impact**: Research shows monitoring disproportionately affects:
- LGBTQ+ students (often "outed" to parents/staff)
- Black and Hispanic students
- Low-income students (more reliant on school devices)
- Students in rural areas

**Chills speech**: Students report self-censoring, not seeking help, and avoiding discussing personal issues because of monitoring.

### Parent Rights Under FERPA

The Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (FERPA) gives you:

| Right | What It Means |
|-------|---------------|
| **Access** | You can see your child's education records |
| **Amendment** | You can request corrections to inaccurate records |
| **Consent** | School generally needs consent to share records |
| **Complaint** | You can file complaints with Dept. of Education |

**Surveillance footage** may become an "education record" if it's used to discipline your specific child, giving you access rights.

### What Parents Can Do

```
SCHOOL DEVICE SAFETY CHECKLIST
==============================

□ Assume everything on school device is monitored
□ Give child personal device for private communication
□ Teach children monitoring exists (age-appropriate)
□ Use personal email/accounts for sensitive topics
□ Mental health support: use personal device or phone
□ LGBTQ+ resources: access on personal device only
□ Ask school: What software do you use? What's monitored?
□ Request opt-out from non-essential monitoring (may be denied)
□ Review school's acceptable use policy
□ Know what triggers alerts and where they go
```

## Protecting Kids' Digital Privacy

### Device Setup for Children

#### Under 13 (COPPA Protected)

| Platform | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| **YouTube** | Use YouTube Kids; disable search |
| **Google** | Use Family Link; supervised account |
| **Apple** | Use Screen Time; disable unneeded features |
| **Social media** | Most ban under-13; don't create accounts |
| **Gaming** | Enable parental controls; disable chat |

#### Teenagers (13-17)

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Privacy settings** | Review all social media privacy settings together |
| **Location sharing** | Disable or limit to trusted family only |
| **Password hygiene** | Unique passwords; consider password manager |
| **Two-factor auth** | Enable on all important accounts |
| **Contact sharing** | Don't share phone number publicly |
| **Photo metadata** | Strip location data before posting |

### Family Conversation Topics

Talk with your children about:

1. **What monitoring exists** (school, apps, parents)
2. **Why privacy matters** (not about hiding wrong things)
3. **Digital footprint** (posts are permanent)
4. **Stranger danger online** (grooming tactics)
5. **When to come to you** (threats, uncomfortable situations)
6. **Their rights** (they have some privacy rights too)

## Social Media Safety

### Platform-Specific Risks

| Platform | Main Risks | Settings to Check |
|----------|------------|-------------------|
| **TikTok** | Location, facial data, strangers | Private account, restricted DMs |
| **Instagram** | Location tags, DM from strangers | Private, restrict comments |
| **Snapchat** | Location (Snap Map), disappearing = false security | Ghost Mode ON |
| **Discord** | Strangers in servers, grooming | Disable DMs from server members |
| **Gaming (Roblox, etc.)** | In-game chat, predators | Disable chat or restrict |

### Red Flags for Grooming

Teach children to recognize:

- Adults asking to keep conversations secret
- Requests for photos (especially private)
- Excessive compliments and attention
- Offering gifts, money, or game currency
- Trying to isolate from friends/family
- Asking to move to different platform
- Questions about being home alone

### What To Do If Something Happens

```
IF YOUR CHILD IS CONTACTED BY A PREDATOR
========================================

1. STAY CALM - Don't blame your child

2. PRESERVE EVIDENCE
   • Screenshot conversations
   • Don't delete anything
   • Note usernames, platforms, times

3. REPORT
   • CyberTipline: CyberTipline.org or 1-800-843-5678
   • Platform's reporting tool
   • Local police (for imminent threats)

4. SUPPORT YOUR CHILD
   • Reassure them it's not their fault
   • Consider professional counseling
   • Don't remove all internet access as punishment
```

## Immigrant Family Considerations

### ICE and Children

**Your child has rights regardless of immigration status:**

- Right to remain silent
- Right against unreasonable searches
- Right to education (Plyler v. Doe - cannot be denied)
- Right not to be used to locate parents

### Preparing Children for ICE Encounters

Age-appropriate preparation:

```
WHAT CHILDREN SHOULD KNOW
=========================

FOR YOUNGER CHILDREN (Under 10):
• Memorize emergency contact's phone number
• Know full name and birthdate
• Know it's okay to say "I need to call my mom/dad"
• Practice: "I'm a child. I want to talk to [contact name]"

FOR OLDER CHILDREN/TEENS:
• Know their rights (remain silent, don't sign)
• Have emergency contact memorized
• Know CORRN hotline: 844-864-8341
• Know not to open door for ICE
• Know to ask "Do you have a warrant signed by a judge?"
• Know to call emergency contact immediately
```

### Family Emergency Plan

```
FAMILY SAFETY PLAN TEMPLATE
===========================

EMERGENCY CONTACTS (Memorize & carry written):
1. ________________________________ (Primary)
2. ________________________________ (Backup)
3. CORRN: 844-864-8341

IF PARENT IS DETAINED:
• Who will care for children? _______________
• School pickup authorization? _______________
• Power of attorney signed? □ Yes □ No
• Important documents location: _______________

CHILDREN KNOW:
□ Full name and birthdate
□ Emergency contacts by heart
□ Not to open door for strangers
□ Basic rights (stay silent, ask for lawyer)
□ Where important papers are kept
```

### School and ICE

**ICE should not come to schools**. Colorado and federal guidance treat schools as "sensitive locations."

If ICE appears at school:
1. School should NOT facilitate access to students
2. School should contact parents immediately
3. School should request warrant
4. Document everything that happens

**Tell your school**: You do not consent to your child being questioned by immigration authorities without your presence and a lawyer.

## Teen-Specific Digital Security

### Why Teens Face Unique Risks

| Risk | Why Teens Are Vulnerable |
|------|--------------------------|
| **Sextortion** | Peer pressure, developing judgment |
| **Doxing** | Online conflicts escalate |
| **Cyberbullying** | Social dynamics play out online |
| **Account takeover** | Password sharing with "friends" |
| **Digital footprint** | Posts affect future college/jobs |

### Sextortion Prevention

**What it is**: Threat to share intimate images unless victim sends more/money.

**Prevention**:
- Never share intimate images (even with partners)
- Use disappearing messages if sexting (still risky)
- Don't accept requests from strangers
- Cover webcam when not in use

**If it happens**:
1. Don't pay or send more
2. Screenshot threats
3. Report to CyberTipline.org
4. Report to platform
5. Tell a trusted adult
6. Know it's NOT their fault

### Safe Communication for Teens

| Need | Recommendation |
|------|----------------|
| **Private messaging** | Signal (disappearing messages) |
| **Sensitive topics** | Personal device, not school |
| **LGBTQ+ resources** | Private browsing, personal device |
| **Mental health support** | Crisis Text Line: Text HOME to 741741 |
| **Organizing/activism** | Signal group, pseudonymous accounts |

## Family Tech Setup Guide

### Recommended Family Security Stack

```
FAMILY DIGITAL SECURITY SETUP
=============================

FOR THE WHOLE FAMILY:
□ Password manager (Bitwarden - free)
□ VPN for public WiFi (ProtonVPN free tier)
□ Signal for family group chat
□ Backup email for emergencies (ProtonMail)

FOR PARENTS:
□ Keep your devices secure (model behavior)
□ Know how to use privacy settings
□ Have honest conversations about monitoring

FOR CHILDREN'S DEVICES:
□ Privacy settings reviewed together
□ Location sharing limited to family
□ Social media accounts private
□ Screen time limits (health, not surveillance)
□ Personal device for personal matters

FOR SCHOOL DEVICES:
□ Assume fully monitored
□ School work only
□ No personal accounts logged in
□ No sensitive searches
```

### Age-Appropriate Privacy Settings

| Age | Device Setup | Monitoring Level |
|-----|--------------|------------------|
| **Under 8** | Supervised use only, no accounts | Full parental control |
| **8-12** | Shared family device, limited apps | Active monitoring, open conversations |
| **13-15** | Personal device, privacy settings together | Check-ins, trust building |
| **16-17** | Increasing autonomy, teach responsibility | Emergency access only, respect privacy |
