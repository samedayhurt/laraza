# First Amendment Protest Rights in Colorado

Your rights to assemble, protest, and speak freely in Colorado - and what to do when police try to limit them.

---

## Your Constitutional Rights

The First Amendment protects:
- **Freedom of Speech** - Express your views
- **Freedom of Assembly** - Gather with others
- **Freedom to Petition** - Demand government action
- **Freedom of the Press** - Document and report

**These rights apply to everyone in the United States**, regardless of citizenship or immigration status.

---

## Where You Can Protest

### Public Forums (Strongest Protection)

```
TRADITIONAL PUBLIC FORUMS
=========================

✓ Public sidewalks
✓ Public parks
✓ Plazas in front of government buildings
✓ Streets (with some restrictions)

These spaces have the HIGHEST First Amendment protection.
Government can only impose "time, place, manner" restrictions
that are content-neutral and leave alternative channels open.
```

### Limited Public Forums

| Location | Your Rights | Restrictions |
|----------|-------------|--------------|
| **Government building lobbies** | Can protest | May have hours/registration |
| **Public university grounds** | Strong protection | Designated areas possible |
| **Public libraries** | Limited speech | Must not disrupt services |
| **City council meetings** | Public comment period | Time limits apply |

### Private Property

| Location | Your Rights |
|----------|-------------|
| **Shopping malls** | Owner can ask you to leave |
| **Private businesses** | No First Amendment protection |
| **Private parking lots** | Can be removed for trespassing |

**Key Exception**: In Colorado, private property open to the public (like malls) may have some petition rights - consult ACLU for specific situations.

## Permits and Your Rights

### When Permits Are Required

| Activity | Permit Needed? |
|----------|----------------|
| **Individual protest on sidewalk** | NO |
| **Small group (under 25) on sidewalk** | Usually NO |
| **Marching in the street** | Recommended but often not required |
| **Large gatherings in parks** | May be required for reserved space |
| **Sound amplification** | Often required |
| **Street closures** | YES |

### If You Don't Have a Permit

**You don't need a permit to march on sidewalks** as long as you:
- Don't block pedestrian traffic completely
- Keep moving or leave space for others to pass
- Don't obstruct building entrances

**Police can ask you to move** to maintain traffic flow, but cannot order you to disperse simply for protesting.

### Permit Denial

If your permit is denied:
1. **Request written reason** for denial
2. **Appeal immediately** - there are usually short deadlines
3. **Contact ACLU**: 303-777-5482
4. **Consider proceeding** - an unconstitutional denial doesn't make your protest illegal

## Dispersal Orders

### When Police Can Legally Disperse a Protest

Under Colorado law (C.R.S. § 18-9-105 and SB21-031), police may ONLY order dispersal when:

```
LEGAL DISPERSAL REQUIREMENTS
============================

□ Imminent threat of force or violence
□ Personal injury is about to occur
□ Significant property damage is imminent
□ Traffic is seriously obstructed

NOT valid reasons for dispersal:
✗ "Unlawful assembly" without specific threat
✗ Chanting, signs, or loud speech
✗ Police discomfort or inconvenience
✗ Counter-protesters present
✗ Blocking some (not all) sidewalk space
```

### Requirements for Valid Dispersal Order

If police issue a dispersal order, they MUST:

1. **Give clear, audible notice** of the order
2. **Explain why** dispersal is being ordered
3. **State how much time** you have to leave
4. **Explain consequences** of not complying
5. **Provide clear exit routes** that are unobstructed

### Your Rights During Dispersal

| You CAN | You CANNOT |
|---------|------------|
| Ask for time to comply | Ignore a lawful order |
| Request clear exit routes | Physically resist arrest |
| Continue recording | Block the exit route |
| Leave at your own pace | Stay if threat is genuine |
| Challenge legality later | |

### If Trapped (Kettling)

"Kettling" is when police surround protesters and prevent exit:

1. **Document everything** - time, location, officers involved
2. **Clearly request exit** - "Which way can we leave?"
3. **Stay calm** - don't give them justification
4. **Note if exits are blocked** - this may make dispersal order unlawful
5. **Get witness contacts** for later legal action

## Interacting with Police

### What To Say

| Situation | Your Response |
|-----------|---------------|
| "Move back" | Comply if reasonable; ask "Where should I go?" |
| "You can't be here" | "This is a public space. Am I being detained?" |
| "Show me ID" | In CO, no requirement unless lawfully detained |
| "What's your name?" | You don't have to answer (5th Amendment) |
| "Disperse now" | "What is the exit route?" Then leave if lawful order |
| "You're under arrest" | Don't resist; say "I do not consent. I want a lawyer." |

### What NOT To Do

- **Don't run** - can be seen as flight
- **Don't argue** - save it for court
- **Don't touch officers** - even incidentally
- **Don't destroy evidence** - keep recordings
- **Don't resist arrest** - even if wrongful

### Documentation Is Key

While protesting:
- **Wear or carry legal observer contact**
- **Know the NLG hotline**: Check nlg.org for local number
- **Write important info on your arm**: Lawyer number, emergency contact
- **Buddy system**: Know someone who will notice if you're arrested

## Colorado-Specific Protections

### SB21-031 (Limits on Government Responses to Protests)

This 2021 Colorado law prohibits government from:

- Ordering dispersal unless **imminent threat** of force/violence
- Deeming protest "unlawful" without **specific threat of injury or property damage**
- Using **chemical agents or projectiles** on peaceful protesters
- Engaging in **mass arrests** without individual probable cause

### Qualified Immunity Limitations

Colorado's police accountability law (SB20-217) removed qualified immunity for state civil rights violations, making it easier to sue officers who violate your rights.

### Recording Rights (See separate guide)

Colorado explicitly protects your right to record police. They cannot:
- Order you to stop recording
- Seize your device without warrant
- Delete recordings
- Retaliate for recording

## If You're Arrested

### Immediate Steps

```
ARREST PROTOCOL
===============

1. STAY CALM - Don't physically resist

2. ASSERT YOUR RIGHTS:
   • "I am exercising my right to remain silent."
   • "I do not consent to any searches."
   • "I want to speak to a lawyer."

3. REMEMBER:
   • Badge numbers / names
   • Time and location
   • Witnesses present
   • What you were doing when arrested

4. DO NOT:
   • Answer questions beyond identifying info
   • Sign anything
   • Make statements
   • Consent to searches

5. AFTER RELEASE:
   • Write down everything immediately
   • Photograph any injuries
   • Get witness contact info
   • Contact lawyer / ACLU
```

### Common Protest-Related Charges

| Charge | What It Means | Severity |
|--------|---------------|----------|
| **Failure to disperse** | Didn't leave after order | Class 2 misdemeanor |
| **Obstructing traffic** | Blocking vehicles | Class 2 misdemeanor |
| **Trespassing** | Remaining on private property | Class 3 misdemeanor |
| **Disorderly conduct** | Broad catch-all charge | Class 1 misdemeanor |
| **Interference with police** | Physical interference | Class 2 misdemeanor |
| **Assault on officer** | Physical contact | Class 4 felony |

### Bail and Release

- **Cite and release**: Often used for minor protest charges
- **Cash bail**: May be required for misdemeanors
- **Bail fund contacts**: NLG chapters often coordinate

## Counter-Protesters and Conflict

### Your Rights

- You can protest near counter-protesters
- Police should maintain buffer zones
- You don't have to engage with counter-protesters

### Police Responsibilities

Police must:
- Protect BOTH sides' speech rights
- Prevent physical confrontations
- Not favor one viewpoint over another

### De-escalation Tips

- **Don't engage** with provocateurs
- **Move away** from confrontational individuals
- **Alert legal observers** to potential violence
- **Document** threats or attacks

### Emergency Contacts

| Organization | Phone | When to Call |
|--------------|-------|--------------|
| **ACLU Colorado** | 303-777-5482 | Rights violations, arrests |
| **NLG National** | 212-679-5100 | Legal observer support |
| **Colorado Legal Services** | 719-545-6708 | Low-income legal help |

### Before Protest

1. **Write lawyer number on arm** in permanent marker
2. **Tell someone** where you'll be and when to expect you back
3. **Arrange jail support**: Someone to track arrestees, bring bail money
4. **Know your local NLG chapter** or legal observer team

### Legal Observer Programs

Legal observers are trained volunteers who:
- Document police behavior
- Track arrests
- Serve as witnesses
- Connect arrestees with lawyers

**To request legal observers** for your event, contact:
- NLG Colorado Chapter
- ACLU of Colorado
- Local civil rights organizations

## Special Situations

### Protests at ICE Facilities

All First Amendment rights apply, BUT:
- Federal property may have additional restrictions
- CBP has broader search powers near borders
- Document everything for later legal action

### Protests at Private Businesses

- 1st Amendment doesn't apply on private property
- Owner can ask you to leave
- Stay on public sidewalk/street
- Don't block entrances

### School Protests (K-12)

Students have limited 1st Amendment rights:
- Cannot substantially disrupt education
- Can be disciplined for walkouts
- Cannot be punished for viewpoint alone
- Tinker v. Des Moines protects political speech

### University Protests

Stronger protections than K-12:
- Public universities are limited public forums
- Private universities set own rules
- Check specific campus policies
- 2024 encampment cases are being litigated
