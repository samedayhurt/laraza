# Ubiquitous Technical Surveillance for Civilians

_Adapted from internal training decks and rewritten for community journalists, organizers, and residents who need to stay safe without military resources._

## 1. Financial Exhaust
Modern commerce tags every swipe with location, time, device ID, and merchant metadata.

**How it tracks you**
- Bank, credit, and payment-app data is resold through data brokers and often subpoenaed in bulk.
- Loyalty cards and gas pumps pair transactions with license plate readers (LPR) in retail parking lots.
- Peer-to-peer apps auto-populate social graphs based on contacts and proximity.

**Civilian countermeasures**
1. Keep community work finances separate from personal accounts—use credit-union prepaid cards funded with cash.
2. Opt out of loyalty programs or create purpose-built aliases unlinked to government IDs.
3. Download statements monthly, redact PII, and store offline so you can see the same pattern an adversary would.

## 2. Physical & Visual Sensors
Cameras have moved from police-only to neighborhood doorbells, traffic grids, mall analytics, and HOA license plate readers.

**Common risks in Pueblo neighborhoods**
- Doorbells such as Ring, Arlo, and Nest are often federated into "law enforcement portals" that can request footage without a warrant.
- Grocery stores and public buildings deploy AI-powered cameras that score "dwell time" and recognized faces.
- Drone footage and news helicopters publish raw imagery that can be mined for presence at actions.

**Civilian mitigations**
1. Map camera clusters by walking/rolling through your neighborhood with a note-taking app—doorbell density matters as much as public CCTV.
2. Use wardrobe layers and posture changes rather than obvious disguises; consistency beats novelty.
3. Schedule rallies and canvassing with "blind in/out" routes: enter the area via one corridor, leave using another.

## 3. Mobility & Travel Metadata
Transit cards, rideshare receipts, toll bills, and ALPR hits form a continuous log.

**Common exposure points**
- Transit agencies retain smart-card tap data for months; some, like RTD Denver, respond quickly to law enforcement requests.
- Car insurance telematics dongles (Progressive Snapshot, State Farm Drive Safe) broadcast GPS + driving behavior every few seconds.
- Rideshare apps tag pickup/dropoff GPS, payment method, and even your phone’s Wi-Fi/Bluetooth scans.

**Mitigations**
1. Keep event travel on community-provided shuttles or carpools that pay cash for fuel and parking.
2. If you must drive, disable telematics, cover license plates when parked on private property, and avoid publicly listed staging areas.
3. Rotate transit cards and consider single-use paper tickets when available.

## 4. Devices & Communications
Phones, laptops, and wearables leak constant telemetry: Wi-Fi probes, Bluetooth beacons, crash reports, and push-notification logs.

**Key lessons from prior operations**
- Treat every sensor (microphone, camera, accelerometer) as a potential witness.
- Cloud backups replicate sensitive chats onto provider infrastructure.
- Messaging apps with "typing" indicators and read receipts provide timeline data even when content is encrypted.

**Steps for civilians**
1. Use the `comms/` workflows: Signal/Session with disappearing messages, Tor/VPN layered browsing, and encrypted Syncthing shares.
2. Carry a "public phone" for logistics and keep your primary device powered down when entering sensitive meetings.
3. Audit mobile OS settings quarterly—reset ad IDs, revoke background location, and delete unused apps en masse.

## 5. Social & Commercial Data Brokers
Advertising IDs, voter files, subscription databases, and breached credentials are accessible to anyone willing to pay brokers.

**Why it matters locally**
- Political consultants in Colorado purchase voter rolls plus appended cell numbers for text banking; those same lists can leak protest rosters.
- Family locator apps silently share geofenced visits (church, clinic, union hall) with marketing partners.
- "People search" engines make it trivial to cross-reference relatives, addresses, and court history.

**Reduce the footprint**
1. File opt-out requests with major brokers (LexisNexis, Acxiom, Epsilon, etc.) and document confirmations in your Obsidian vault.
2. Use alias emails via SimpleLogin / Firefox Relay for petitions or newsletter signups.
3. Build a habit of redacting faces, license plates, and addresses from every media asset before posting.

## Quick Checklist Before Field Work
- Cash on hand for transit/food.
- Burner note-taking device synced via encrypted workflow.
- Updated camera map + ingress/egress routes.
- Confirmed fallback channel (LoRa/APRS or phone tree) if cellular service is disrupted.

Civilian resilience is about consistency, not gadgets. Pick a small set of practices, document them in this repo, and teach them to the next neighbor.
