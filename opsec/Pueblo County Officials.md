# Pueblo County Elected Officials (October 2025)

This briefing consolidates the currently seated countywide officials referenced in the [Pueblo County government roster on Wikipedia][1] and pairs them with recent reporting about their party affiliation, professional interests, and noteworthy decisions or controversies. The focus is on offices that directly steward public money, records, justice, or land.

> **How to use this:** before a protest or outreach push, identify which elected office can de-escalate harassment (e.g., Clerk’s office for voter suppression, Sheriff for jail conditions). Bring the “business interests” column to meetings so you can surface conflicts of interest on the spot.

## Countywide Offices

| Office | Name & Party | Business / Financial Interests | Notable Decisions / Positions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clerk & Recorder | **Candace Rivera (Democrat)** | Former federal contractor and small-business owner before running the 60-person Clerk’s office.[10] | Doubled the number of bipartisan signature-verification judges to deliver the county’s fastest election canvass in recent history (final unofficial results within ~6 hours).[9] |
| Treasurer & Public Trustee | **Kim Archuleta (Democrat)** | First-generation CSU–Pueblo business graduate; career county finance employee whose office runs the annual tax-lien sale.[11][8] | Oversees the 2025 tax-lien sale and online bidder registration, keeping the sale open to institutional and local investors via the treasurer’s portal.[8][11] |
| Assessor | **Frank Beltran (Democrat)** | Longtime assessor; biography emphasizes multi-generational Pueblo roots and decades inside the assessor’s office rather than outside business holdings.[8][12] | Led the 2023–24 reassessment cycle that produced steep valuation hikes on vacant land, prompting property-tax outreach and protest clinics described on the assessor’s public site.[12] |
| Sheriff | **David Lucero (Democrat)** | Career law-enforcement officer; no private company disclosed. | Filed a December 2024 lawsuit arguing the state’s public-sector collective bargaining law should not bind sheriff’s deputies, asserting his office is constitutionally independent from county government.[7] |
| District Attorney (10th Judicial District) | **Kala Beauvais (Republican)** | Career prosecutor; chief trial deputy before running for DA.[15] | Won office by defeating Democrat Kyle Aber in 2024 and has pledged to continue “fighting for justice” as the county’s first female DA, highlighting case backlogs and violent-crime prosecutions.[15] |
| Coroner* | **Brian Cotter (Republican)** | Family owns Davis Mortuary and Cotter Family Funeral Services.[13] | Under criminal investigation after inspectors found 24 decomposing bodies and containers of remains hidden inside Davis Mortuary in 2025; commissioners appointed an interim coroner while the probe continues.[13] |
| Surveyor | **Randy Reeves (Democrat)** | Co-owner/vice president of Cardinal Points Surveying and decades-long land surveyor in southern Colorado.[14] | Continues to perform contract surveying via Cardinal Points while serving as the elected surveyor since 2010; county bio lists over 30 years of private-sector work.[14] |

\*Brian Cotter submitted his resignation after the Davis Mortuary investigation; interim appointments are in place, but the elected term technically runs through 2026.[13]
